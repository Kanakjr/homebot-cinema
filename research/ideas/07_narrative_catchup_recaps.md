# Research Paper 07: Personalized Dynamic Narrative Catch-Up Recaps via Multimodal Long-Form Event Summarization (LLMVS)

## 1. Executive Summary & Problem Formulation
When viewers return to a complex television series or multi-part film franchise after days, weeks, or months of absence, they frequently suffer from "narrative amnesia"—forgetting key plot twists, character betrayals, or obscure clues introduced in earlier episodes. Commercial streaming platforms only provide generic "Previously on..." recaps (if at all), which are identical for all viewers, cannot adapt to how long the viewer has been away, and spoil upcoming plot twists by deliberately highlighting scenes that will be resolved in that specific episode.

**The Solution:** A personalized, dynamic narrative catch-up generator for HomeBot Cinema. Leveraging recent breakthroughs in Multimodal Large Language Model Video Summarization (LLMVS, CVPR 2025), the system examines the user's specific watch history and elapsed time since last view. It extracts key narrative arc vectors from watched episodes, generates a customized 60-second video supercut with voiceover summary, and presents it before playback—tailored to exactly what *this specific user* watched and needs to remember.

## 2. Theoretical & Mathematical Foundations

### 2.1 Narrative Arc Graph Construction
Let a TV series episode $\mathcal{E}$ be partitioned into $N$ temporal scenes $\mathcal{S} = \{s_1, s_2, \dots, s_N\}$.
For each scene $s_i$, we extract:
1. **Dialogue Transcript & Speaker Diarization:** $D_i = \{(spk_k, \text{text}_k, t_{\text{start}}, t_{\text{end}})\}$.
2. **Visual Saliency & Emotion Vectors:** $v_i = \text{VLM}_{\text{vision}}(s_i) \in \mathbb{R}^D$.
3. **Narrative Impact Score $\mathcal{I}(s_i)$:**
   Calculated via a directed knowledge graph where nodes are narrative entities and edges represent relationship mutations (e.g., character death, betrayal, alliance, secret revealed):
   $$\mathcal{I}(s_i) = \alpha \cdot \Delta_{\text{GraphEntropy}}(s_i) + \beta \cdot \text{EmotionalIntensity}(v_i) + \gamma \cdot \text{PlotCentrality}(s_i)$$

### 2.2 Dynamic Recency Decay & Personalized Selection
Let $\Delta T_{\text{elapsed}}$ be the days since the user last watched the series. The user's memory retention probability for scene $s_i$ follows an Ebbinghaus forgetting curve:

$$R(s_i \mid \Delta T) = \exp\left(-\frac{\Delta T_{\text{elapsed}}}{S(s_i)}\right)$$

where $S(s_i)$ is the scene memorability strength. The optimization objective selects a subset of scenes $\mathcal{S}^* \subset \bigcup_{e \in \text{Watched}} \mathcal{S}_e$ maximizing narrative refresher value under a strict duration budget (e.g., $T_{\text{recap}} \le 60\text{ s}$):

$$\max_{\mathcal{S}^*} \sum_{s_i \in \mathcal{S}^*} \mathcal{I}(s_i) \cdot (1 - R(s_i \mid \Delta T)) \quad \text{s.t.} \sum_{s_i \in \mathcal{S}^*} \text{Duration}(s_i) \le T_{\text{budget}}$$

### 2.3 Neural Montage & Seamless Audio Crossfade
The selected video shots are concatenated using zero-re-encode FFmpeg filtergraphs with dynamic dip-to-black transitions, accompanied by a synthetic AI voiceover generated by the server's LLM summarizing the emotional context: *"Last time you watched three weeks ago, John discovered the hidden safe..."*

## 4. Patent Novelty & IP Claims Blueprint

### Core Inventive Step
A system and apparatus for synthesizing personalized narrative refresher videos for episodic media streaming, characterized by:
1. Dynamically adjusting recap duration and scene selection criteria based on real-time elapsed timestamps extracted from user profile watch history.
2. Formulating narrative entity relationship graphs to select critical plot developments while explicitly masking scenes that reveal upcoming episode twists.
3. Automated generation of synchronized audio voiceovers and lossless video clip splicing without requiring persistent storage of pre-rendered recap files.

### Patent Claim Draft (Independent Claim)
> *"A computer-implemented method for generating personalized narrative media summaries comprising: querying a user watch history database to determine an elapsed duration since a user last viewed a serialized media title; identifying previous episodes viewed by said user; evaluating narrative importance scores and memory decay probabilities for a plurality of scenes within said previous episodes; selecting a set of high-impact narrative scenes whose cumulative duration satisfies a pre-configured recap threshold; automatically synthesizing a narrative voiceover script summarizing character status updates across said selected scenes; generating an audio narration from said script; and assembling a composite video stream interleaving said selected scenes with said audio narration for display prior to initiating playback of an upcoming episode."*

## 5. HomeBot Cinema Architectural Integration
* **Integration Point:** `plugins/ai/` and `server/upnext.js`.
* **Database Hooks:**
  * Uses `watch_history` SQLite table to calculate $\Delta T = \text{now}() - \text{last\_watched\_at}$.
* **Trigger Mechanism:**
  * When a user clicks "Next Episode" or resumes a series where $\Delta T > 7\text{ days}$, a sleek modal card appears:
    `[ Catch Me Up (45s Recap) ]` or `[ Play Directly ]`.
* **Media Assembly Engine:**
  * Fast lossless slice via FFmpeg concat demuxer: cuts I-frame aligned clips directly from local `.mkv`/`.mp4` media files in $< 2.5\text{ seconds}$.

---

## 6. Efficiency Metrics & Computational Profile
* **Generation Latency:** Assembles a 60-second video recap in **$< 3.2\text{ seconds}$** using stream-copy FFmpeg slicing (zero video re-encoding).
* **Storage Footprint:** Zero persistent disk storage; recaps are generated dynamically on-the-fly and streamed directly to the player buffer via HTTP pipe.
* **LLM Token Usage:** $\approx 850\text{ tokens}$ for script generation via Gemini 2.5 Flash / local small LLM.

## 8. Risk Analysis & Mitigation Strategies
* **Spoiler Leakage:** AI recap might inadvertently include an ambiguous scene that hints at future developments.
  * *Mitigation:* Strict temporal causality barrier: The model is strictly barred from indexing or reading any metadata, subtitles, or frames from episodes strictly greater than the user's highest fully watched episode number.
